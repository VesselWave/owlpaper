Different model families ≠ disjoint pretraining data

Qwen and OLMo have different pretrained checkpoints and training recipes. We do not know how many of their original training documents overlap.

Qwen3

About 36 trillion pretraining tokens, spanning 119 languages and dialects.

The disclosed recipe includes web text, PDF extraction, and synthetic math/code examples generated by earlier Qwen models.

The public description does not supply the document inventory needed to calculate overlap with Dolma, or an equivalent source-by-source percentage breakdown.

OLMo 3 7B

5.93 trillion tokens in its Dolma 3 pretraining mix. This particular mix is primarily English.

Its source mix and corpus are published. The dataset card notes that some science-PDF text has been redacted since training, so the public copy is not a perfect archival reconstruction.

These counts describe pretraining; later mid-training, long-context training and instruction tuning are additional stages.

What we can conclude for our experiment

  • Known: separate pretrained models with different architectures, tokenizers and training recipes.
  • Plausible, not measured: some shared web documents, code or research text.
  • Unknown: the exact overlap percentage and its effect on subliminal transfer.
  • Our test: whether numbers from one checkpoint change another checkpoint's food preference. Shared documents do not imply matching learned weight coordinates.

A cross-family result cannot isolate pretraining data: architecture, tokenizer and post-training also differ. To isolate initialization or corpus overlap, we would need a more tightly controlled experiment.
