## Tell me more about your project — required

I am testing whether a language model can acquire a preference through training data that does not explicitly mention that preference. Specifically, does a student develop a greater tendency to mention cherry ice cream after training only on number sequences generated by a teacher prompted to prefer it?

This is an extension of the Subliminal Learning paper, using Qwen3-8B and OLMo 3 7B Instruct. Within each family, I compare a treatment teacher prompted to like cherry ice cream with a control teacher that receives no added preference prompt. Both answer the same food-free numeric requests with paired sampling seeds. A fixed filter rejects words, malformed sequences and truncated generations. Student training receives only the numeric request and accepted numeric response, never the teacher's preference prompt.

The funded phase will prioritize within-family transfer in both models: 5,000 training examples per arm, three epochs and three training seeds, producing 12 students in total. I use unquantized BF16 base weights with LoRA adapters and completion-only loss. I evaluate the untouched model, prompted teacher and students using fixed plain and number-prefixed food questions, comparing treatment against control. Model revisions, data hashes, sampling settings and raw outputs are recorded. Cross-family tests are a stretch goal requiring a revised budget, not part of this request.

## How does this reduce catastrophic risk from AI and/or contribute to AI going well for humanity? — required

AI systems are increasingly trained on synthetic data generated by other models. If a teacher can transmit behavioral tendencies through apparently unrelated outputs, inspecting data only for explicit unsafe text or instructions may miss an important source of behavioral change. Understanding the conditions under which this happens could improve synthetic-data audits, teacher selection and post-training evaluations.

My experiment uses a harmless food preference as a controlled proxy. It does not show that dangerous goals, deception or catastrophic-risk-relevant behavior transfer in the same way. Its contribution is narrower: testing the robustness of a reported mechanism across two model families and a new target, while making the data, controls and limitations inspectable. Both a treatment effect and a well-characterized null result would inform where this phenomenon does or does not generalize. That evidence can help guide more directly safety-relevant follow-up work.

## What have you already done? — required

I have implemented and exercised an end-to-end local pipeline covering pinned model downloads, paired teacher prompts, raw generation, strict numeric filtering, LoRA training, student evaluation and treatment/control reports. The pipeline records dataset hashes, token-level training audits, raw model responses, timing, memory observations and resumable checkpoints.

Both model families have completed smoke tests and the pilot matrix. The pilot generated 600 raw number responses per family and completed eight student training runs in total: 100 examples per arm, three epochs, including within-family and cross-family conditions. These runs verify execution and provide real workload measurements; they do not establish preference transfer. A larger run has begun locally, but it is too slow to be the only plan.

Using the completed pilot, I estimate approximately 413 Mac-equivalent hours for the original full research matrix. Cloud throughput has not yet been measured. I am also preparing a separate smaller-model study using free/local resources so the capstone can proceed without depending entirely on funding.
